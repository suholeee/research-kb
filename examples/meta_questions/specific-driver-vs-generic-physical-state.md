# Do Specific Drivers Act Directly, or Mainly Through a Generic Physical State?

## Systems Involved
- Chromatin dynamics from [chromatin-dynamics-active-vs-passive-control](../questions/chromatin-dynamics-active-vs-passive-control.md)
- Chromatin packing-transcription coupling from [chromatin-packing-transcription-directionality](../questions/chromatin-packing-transcription-directionality.md)
- Chromatin loop-extrusion/topology coupling from [loop-extrusion-torsion-architecture](../questions/loop-extrusion-torsion-architecture.md)
- Membrane spacing-controlled alignment from [spacing-controlled-raft-alignment](../questions/spacing-controlled-raft-alignment.md)

## Shared Structure
- Each system contains a competition between a driver-specific model and a more generic physical-state model.
- In chromatin dynamics, the competition is between active transcription-linked or ATP-dependent driving and passive compaction-dependent material state.
- In packing-transcription coupling, the competition is between transcription as a direct structural organizer and packing as an upstream physical regulator.
- In loop-extrusion/topology coupling, the competition is between a driver-specific SMC motor property, such as generated linking-number change, and a broader architectural state set by loading, residence, barriers, topoisomerases, and chromatin buffering.
- In membranes, the competition is between `Ca2+`-specific chemistry and a generic state model centered on spacing or hydration-controlled coupling.

## Differences
- In chromatin, active biological processes may remain ongoing nonequilibrium drivers even after part of the physical state is set, and one matched readout may not align motion amplitude, motion coherence, and transcription simultaneously.
- The loop-extrusion branch makes this distinction sharper: matching generic compaction or even cohesin occupancy may miss driver-specific motor chemistry if torsion generation or relaxation directly controls loop growth.
- In membranes, ion specificity may matter mainly through how it changes intermembrane distance or interfacial-water structure, with less evidence that chemistry continues to drive alignment once the operative membrane state is truly matched.
- Chromatin therefore has a stronger active-versus-passive tension, whereas the membrane system has a stronger specific-chemistry-versus-proxy-state tension.

## Unified Hypothesis
- Specific drivers often matter because they place the system into a particular mesoscale physical state, but direct driver-specific effects remain detectable when sufficiently rich matched-state comparisons fail.
- The generic physical state explains most steady-state organization only when the matched state is close to the operative variable set; otherwise driver-specific effects can persist because the measured state was incomplete rather than because the generic-state view is wrong.

## Testable Predictions
- When two perturbations generate the same sufficiently rich measured structural state, driver-specific differences should shrink if the generic-state model is correct.
- In chromatin, matching compaction or packing state across perturbations should reduce differences, but may not eliminate them if active driving, boundary competence, or timescale-specific dynamics still contribute directly.
- In chromatin loop-architecture experiments, matching SMC occupancy and boundary placement should still leave residual loop-size or insulation differences if SMC-generated torsion or topoisomerase handling acts as a direct driver rather than only as a route into a generic architecture state.
- In membranes, matched-spacing conditions produced by different solutes should largely converge only if the relevant hydration profile and path history are also matched; persistent differences at matched `D` alone are not yet enough to prove chemistry-specific driving.
- Persistent differences after matching both proximal state variables and history would identify direct driver-specific contributions rather than state-mediated ones.
